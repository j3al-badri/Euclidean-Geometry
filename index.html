Information

Plane Euclidean Geometry Engine Guide

The Plane Euclidean Geometry Engine is an interactive environment for geometric drawing, construction, and calculation. It allows geometric figures and relationships to be created and studied on a single drawing plane, with tools for calculation, measurement, movement, intersection, locus, image analysis, and the execution of some geometric constructions and proofs.

Line

Line and Line Segment

A straight line is determined by two points and can be extended in both directions. A line segment is the part bounded by two points and has a definite measurable length.

Relations Between Lines

Two lines may be intersecting, parallel, or perpendicular. Their point of intersection and the angle between them determine the nature of their relationship.

Parallel Lines

Parallel lines in a plane do not intersect no matter how far they are extended. Through a point outside a given line, exactly one line can be drawn parallel to it.

Perpendicular Lines

Perpendicular lines intersect at a right angle of 90°. A perpendicular to a line can be drawn from a point on the line or from a point outside it.

Perpendicular Bisector

The perpendicular bisector of a line segment is a line that passes through the midpoint of the segment and is perpendicular to it. Every point on the perpendicular bisector is equidistant from the two endpoints of the segment.

Intersection of Lines

When two lines intersect, they have a common point called the intersection point. The intersection may lie on the drawn portions of the lines or on their extensions.

Angle Between Two Lines

When two lines intersect, four angles are formed. Vertically opposite angles are equal, and every two adjacent angles on a straight line have a sum of 180°.

Types of Angles

An acute angle is less than 90°, a right angle equals 90°, an obtuse angle is greater than 90° and less than 180°, and a straight angle equals 180°.

What Can the Engine Do with a Line?

In the engine, a line can be drawn using two existing points, and the distance between two points, the length of a line segment, and the distance from a point to a line or its extension can be calculated.

The engine can also calculate the angle between two lines, determine their intersection point or the intersection of their extensions, check whether two lines are parallel or perpendicular, and construct perpendicular or parallel lines in the constructions supported by the engine.

Movement, eraser, colors, line styles, arrows, and text tools can also be used on the drawing.

Triangle

Triangle

A triangle is a geometric figure consisting of three vertices, three sides, and three angles. The sum of its interior angles is 180°.

Types of Triangles by Sides

Equilateral: Its three sides are equal and its three angles are 60°.

Isosceles: It has two equal sides, and the angles opposite those sides are equal.

Scalene: Its three side lengths are different.

Types of Triangles by Angles

Acute: All three angles are less than 90°.

Right: It contains an angle of 90°.

Obtuse: It contains an angle greater than 90°.

Triangle Congruence

Congruent triangles are equal in shape and size, and their corresponding sides and angles are equal. Important geometric cases include three sides SSS, two sides and the included angle SAS, and two angles and the included side ASA.

Triangle Similarity

Similar triangles have the same shape, although their sizes may differ. Their corresponding angles are equal and their corresponding side lengths are proportional.

Altitude

A triangle altitude is the perpendicular drawn from a vertex to the opposite side or its extension. The three altitudes intersect at the orthocenter.

Median

A median is a line segment joining a vertex of the triangle to the midpoint of the opposite side. The three medians intersect at the centroid.

Angle Bisector

An angle bisector divides a triangle angle into two equal angles. The internal angle bisectors intersect at the incenter.

Perpendicular Bisector of a Side

The perpendicular bisector passes through the midpoint of a side and is perpendicular to it. The perpendicular bisectors of the triangle's sides intersect at the circumcenter.

Incircle

A circle lying inside the triangle and tangent to its three sides. Its center is the intersection point of the internal angle bisectors.

Circumcircle

A circle passing through the three vertices of the triangle. Its center is the intersection point of the perpendicular bisectors of the triangle's sides.

What Can the Engine Do with a Triangle?

A free triangle can be drawn by defining three vertices, and an equilateral or isosceles triangle can also be constructed.

The engine supports constructing a triangle from three sides SSS, from two sides and the included angle SAS, or from two angles and the included side ASA.

Side lengths, area, perimeter, angles, altitudes, medians, angle bisectors, and triangle centers can be calculated, in addition to the incircle and circumcircle.

The triangle can also be combined with lines, points, circles, and movement, measurement, intersection, and other construction tools.

Circle

Circle

A circle is the set of points in a plane that are at a fixed distance from a point called the center. This fixed distance is called the radius.

Radius

A line segment joining the center of the circle to any point on its circumference.

Diameter

A line segment joining two points on the circumference and passing through the center of the circle. The diameter is twice the radius.

Chord

A line segment joining two points on the circumference of the circle. The diameter is the longest chord of a circle.

Secant

A line that intersects the circle at two points.

Tangent

A line that touches the circle at one point called the point of tangency. The radius drawn to the point of tangency is perpendicular to the tangent.

Tangent from a Point Outside the Circle

Two tangents can be drawn from a point outside a circle to the circle, and the two tangent segments from the external point to the points of tangency are equal in length.

Common Tangents Between Two Circles

Two circles may have common tangents. Their number and type vary according to the positions of the circles, the distance between their centers, and their radii.

Central Angle

An angle whose vertex is the center of the circle and whose sides determine an arc on the circumference.

Circle Through Three Points

Exactly one circle passes through three non-collinear points, and its center lies at the intersection of the perpendicular bisectors of the chords joining those points.

What Can the Engine Do with a Circle?

The engine supports constructing a circle in several ways, including a circle from a center and diameter, a circle through three points, and constructions of a circle tangent to a line or another circle according to the selected operation.

The circle's center, radius, diameter, area, and circumference can be found.

The position of a point relative to the circle can also be studied, supported tangents can be drawn, and the circle can be used with lines, points, triangles, and the intersection, movement, and color tools.

Arc

Arc

An arc is a part of a circle's circumference bounded by two points on the circumference.

Central Angle of an Arc

The central angle corresponding to an arc is the angle whose vertex lies at the center of the circle and whose sides pass through the endpoints of the arc.

Arc Length

The arc length is determined by the radius of the circle and the measure of its corresponding central angle.

What Can the Engine Do with an Arc?

An arc can be drawn using an existing circle and geometric points, and the properties supported by the engine can be calculated, including arc length and central angle, while allowing the arc to be used within the geometric drawing.

Rectangle

Rectangle

A rectangle is a quadrilateral whose four angles are right angles. Each pair of opposite sides is parallel and equal.

Rectangle Diagonals

The diagonals of a rectangle are equal in length, and each bisects the other at their point of intersection.

Area and Perimeter

The area of a rectangle equals length multiplied by width, and its perimeter equals the sum of the lengths of its sides.

What Can the Engine Do with a Rectangle?

A rectangle can be drawn using points, and a special rectangle can also be constructed by entering its width and height. Its supported geometric properties and calculations can be studied, and movement, color, erasing, measurement, and intersection tools can be used with it.

Polygon

Polygon

A polygon is a closed plane figure made of connected line segments. The segments are called sides, and the points where the sides meet are called vertices.

Area and Perimeter

The perimeter of a polygon is the sum of the lengths of its sides, and its area is the measure of the region enclosed within it.

What Can the Engine Do with a Polygon?

A polygon can be drawn using a set of points, and a special polygon can also be constructed by specifying the number of sides and the side length. The geometric properties supported by the engine can be calculated, and movement, color, erasing, and measurement tools can be used with it.

Drawing and Editing Tools

Cursor

Used to control the cursor mode and interact with the drawing plane.

Eraser

Used to erase drawn objects.

Move

Allows points and movable objects to be moved and helps show how related figures and relationships change.

Undo and Redo

Undo reverses the last operation, and Redo restores the operation that was undone.

Labels and Distances

Object labels and distances can be shown or hidden on the drawing to reduce screen clutter when needed.

Axes and Grid

The X and Y axes and the grid can be shown or hidden to make use of the coordinate system while drawing.

Colors

The color tool allows the color of the selected geometric object to be changed and restored to its original color.

Line Styles

The appearance of a line can be changed to thick, thin, dashed, or dash-dot, and a one-way arrow or arrows at both ends can be added.

Text

Text can be written and placed on the drawing plane, with a selectable size and color and geometric symbols such as: ∠ ⟂ ∥ ° ≅ ∼ △ ⇒ π.

Drawing Snapshot

Used to obtain a snapshot of the current drawing plane.

PDF

Allows the drawing to be prepared for printing or saving as a PDF, with selection of paper size and orientation.

Zoom In and Out

Zooming is intended to display the drawing more clearly. For calculations, drawing, or use of drawing tools, it is preferable to return to the original size O.

Plot Function

The Plot Function tool allows a mathematical function to be entered in the form y = f(x), with minimum and maximum limits specified for the X and Y axes. The engine plots the function curve on the drawing plane, and supported operations can be used to study the intersection of the function with some geometric objects such as a line or circle.

Calculation and Construction Tools

Distance

The distance between two existing points on the drawing can be calculated.

Angle Between Two Lines

Calculates the angle between two lines or their geometric directions.

Intersection Point

Used to find the intersection of objects whose intersections the engine supports calculating, including some line, circle, and function cases.

Rotation

The rotation tool allows a figure and a center of rotation to be selected and then the rotation angle of the figure to be specified.

Find

Find is used to query a geometric property using the names of objects that exist in the drawing. Supported operations include a range of distances, lengths, angles, midpoints, intersections, areas, perimeters, circle properties, relations between lines, and point positions.

Draw

The Draw tool is used to execute geometric constructions through text commands based on the names of objects that exist on the drawing plane.

Prove

The Prove tool uses the written givens, the figure in the drawing, and the proof rules supported by the engine to attempt to build a geometric proof. Symbols such as ∠ ⟂ ∥ can be used when writing the givens and the statement to prove.

The proof tool's capability is linked to the rules and theorems supported by the engine; therefore, the presence of the tool does not mean that it can prove every possible geometry problem.

Geometric Locus

What Is a Geometric Locus?

A geometric locus is the set of points that satisfy a specified condition or geometric relationship.

Dynamic Locus in the Engine

A moving point can be linked to a path or geometric object, and another point can then be defined whose position depends geometrically on the moving point.

The engine calculates multiple positions of the dependent point as the moving point changes position, then draws the set of those positions to display the locus. This makes it possible to observe the effect of the geometric relationship dynamically instead of studying the figure at only one position.

Reference Image

The Reference Image tool allows an image to be uploaded and placed behind the geometric drawing. The image's opacity and size can be controlled, adjusted, and then fixed in place.

You can draw over the reference image, which is useful when reconstructing a geometric drawing or comparing the drawing with an existing image or diagram. The reference image does not analyze the image contents; it primarily uses the image as a visual basis for drawing.

Image Analysis

The Image Analysis tool differs from the reference image. It attempts to recognize some geometric objects in an image and convert what is detected into data that the engine can use.

The engine displays the extracted data before drawing it. The user can review it, delete an incorrectly detected object, or add an object that was not detected, and then confirm the data and draw the figure.

Image analysis is an assisting tool, and automatic detection may not be correct for every image; therefore, the data should be reviewed before being accepted.

Reference Image or Image Analysis?

Reference Image: Place the image behind the drawing plane and draw over it.

Image Analysis: Attempt to extract geometric objects from the image and convert them into a drawing that can be used inside the engine.

How to Use the Engine

1. Select the required geometric object and draw it on the drawing plane.

2. Use the drawing and editing tools to move objects, change their appearance, or add text.

3. Use direct calculations or the Find tool to obtain geometric values and relationships.

4. Use Draw to execute supported text-based geometric constructions.

5. Use Geometric Locus to study how the position of a point linked by a geometric relationship changes.

6. Use Prove for problems that fall within the supported proof rules.

7. The Reference Image can be used to draw over an image, or Image Analysis can be used to attempt to convert image elements into geometric data.

Points, lines, triangles, circles, arcs, rectangles, polygons, functions, and the different tools can be combined on one drawing plane to build and study multiple geometric constructions.
